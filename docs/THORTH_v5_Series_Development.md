## 1. Overview

- **Format:** 7 × 20 min limited series (pilot 24–25 min). Period: circa 2006.
- **Logline:** A soul sheltering in a towel protects the boy who stole it — until every homeless spirit in the land begins converging on the one light left burning for the dead.
- **Engines:** *Moral (Tumbbad):* every supernatural gift has a price — paid by a dead man who can't rest. *Narrative (Stranger Things — internal reference only, never a marketing comp):* a lucky towel is the small mystery; an erased burial ground and a country of displaced souls is the hidden world underneath.
- **Theme:** *The dead are dispossessed exactly as the living are.* Dramatized in land, roots, and cloth — never spoken.
- **Format honesty:** limited series; the engine resolves with the tying. The coda's moving thorthu is the anthology door — pure thematic chill, never an explicit hook (locked, §9).

## 2. The Mythology — Rule System v5.1 (locked)

1. **The binding rite.** When someone in this (fictional) community dies, the plathi draws the departing soul into a thorthu, ties the sacred knot, whispers *"until the land accepts you again"*, and hangs the cloth in the **kavu** — the sacred grove over the ancestral **savaparambu**. A hung soul rests. An unhoused soul wanders and weakens. **Every plathi's knot is a signature:** a trained eye reads who tied a cloth, and when — the kavu is not a curtain of cloth but an archive, each thorthu a name.
2. **THE MASTER RULE: the soul consents, not the cloth.** The thorthu goes where the soul wills. It cannot be taken from an unwilling soul nor kept from one that chooses to leave. The boys' theft held because Papa — a protector all his life — *chose* the dying boy. The village cannot recover it by force. The girl cannot snatch it. The finale requires **two releases in the same moment**: Hari ready to let go, Papa ready to rest.
   - **2a. The door opens from the living side (new — squares the Ep6→7 gap).** Untying the first knot does not evict the soul; it withdraws the keeper's claim. Hari's untying is surrender, not expulsion — and Papa, free to go from that moment, *stays for the rite*: the gatherer does not leave before the gathering. Nothing in the dark gap between Ep6 and Ep7 contradicts Rule 2, and the rules bible must keep it that way.
3. **The cloth is shelter, not prison.** Papa is not trapped — he is *housed*. The Ep5 reveal reframes everything: Hari isn't carrying a talisman; he is **living in someone's afterlife**.
4. **Protection has a visible price.** Every save costs Papa strength. Each time the cloth works, it dulls a shade darker — and **a new small knot appears along its edge.** The gang can count the cost; so can we. By Ep6 the thorthu is grey and heavy with knots: a dead man's overtime, written in cotton. **And the ledger has an endpoint:** a hem holds only so many knots — when the edge fills, the protection fails, whatever anyone wills; depletion runs toward a terminal remainder, so that when everything spendable is stripped away, what is left is the first knot — the promise itself. *(Tumbbad law, made prop — see §7 knot bible: canonical counts, one audit shot per episode, absolutist continuity, hem capacity.)*
5. **Papa can leave the cloth — briefly, at night, at great cost.** He steps out only to protect. Karadi will see him (Ep5 confirmation of the Ep4 glimpse): a still white figure at the end of the corridor, standing between the sleeping boys and the dark. *(Director's note: he also walks unseen earlier — hidden deep-background frames, Eps 2–4, §7.)*
6. **The hostel stands on the erased savaparambu**; its kavu was cut at construction. For decades the unbound slept shallow — dogs uneasy, nothing more.
7. **The thorthu is the beacon.** The night the boys carry it through the gate, a housed soul arrives among the homeless — a lamp lit in a dark room. The graveyard wakes. *Everything that follows is Hari's doing.* **And the metaphor is literal biography:** Papa kept a lamp burning for the road every night of his life. His lifelong open door is what summons the homeless dead. The crisis is his virtue. **And every save feeds it:** each time the cloth spends, the beacon brightens a shade — the dogs face B-block a little longer, the whisper carries a little farther. The luck era is not background to the siege; it is the summons.
8. **The unbound are weak: they call, whisper, tilt luck — they cannot act.** They need hands.
9. **The green-eyed spirit is the hands they found — and their kin.** A century-old soul whose desert burial ground was razed for construction; her clan's routes have unknowingly orbited grave-grounds ever since — her search, steering their feet. They passed this land generations ago when the bindings were whole, and nothing called. Now the ground is open and a beacon burns. **Homeless spirits recognize each other.** She is summoned — the graveyard's instrument and its fellow victim.
   - **9a. Why her and not their own (new — closes the agency seam).** A ground cannot spend its own dead: souls sunk in their own earth are *roots*, not hands — the land holds what it grieves. Only a traveling soul, loose above foreign ground, can be lent the ground's strength. The local dead can whisper forever; they can never touch. She can.
10. **The crossroads stakes (stated, then witnessed once).** If the beacon keeps burning over open ground, displaced dead from every erased ground in the country will converge — not evil, homeless. The plathi states it in Ep6; we show its leading edge only — a queue, never an army — and exactly one verifiably far-travelled arrival (§5, Ep6).
    - **10a. Why a queue, never a storm.** Each unhoused soul perceives the beacon as a gate meant only for it, and waits before it, procedure-bound — the dead do not trespass on one another's hope. The queue is the mythology's own crowd discipline: the rules enforce what the director's restraint would otherwise have to.
11. **Resolution: the good soul gathers the lost.** Loose souls cannot be caught; they must be *drawn* — to a good soul, housed and willing, held at the centre. Only a plathi's soul knows how to gather — **and Papa was the plathi before this one.** He performs his life's work one final time. Each gathered soul receives **its own new thorthu** — no cages, one home each — hung in the trees of the living kavu. The girl is not defeated. She is *finally housed.* **The rite's hardest question is jurisdictional:** by what law does a Kerala plathi knot a Rajasthani soul — *"until the land accepts you again,"* when this was never her land? Papa's last act answers it by widening his own rite: **any tended ground is the land.** The Ep7 wavering is this doctrine being decided.

**Permanent ambiguities (bible-locked, never resolved on screen):**
- **Was the luck ever real, or only safety?** Hari is gifted at almost everything and tries nothing; Papa only ever *protected*. No line of dialogue may ever answer this. The Ep1 bite is mundane (the hospital already says so) and is never retro-mystified.
- **Why the first knot survives in Ep7.** Papa's cloth hangs with every knot undone but the plathi's first. The kept promise — *"until the land accepts you again"* — accepted. Never explained aloud.
- **Whose cloth moves in the coda.** Never answered.

## 3. Characters (the five-hand ensemble + the web)

- **PAPA** *(now a character, not only a mythology)* — Karadi's grandfather; the village's previous plathi; a protector all his life. Two habits define him and the season: **his hands tied half the cloths in the kavu**, and **he kept a lamp burning in his window every night for anyone still on the road.** We meet him alive for under a minute (Act Zero) and grieve him for seven episodes. His arc is the show's thesis: the man who housed the dead spends the season housing the living, and is finally allowed to rest.
- **HARI** — Gifted at almost everything; tries nothing. Want: never be seen failing → from Ep2, *stay extraordinary*. Need: act without guarantee. Lie: "the cloth is why we survive" → deeper lie, post-Ep5: *"I deserve this more than a dead man deserves rest."* **New in v5:** one unguarded, cloth-independent kindness in Ep2 (he coaches the hostel's worst singer for the fest heats, expecting nothing) — the fall must fall from somewhere; a **use ladder** (petty → public glory → "generously" on others' behalf → institutional) so Ep6's refusal is inevitable, not sudden; **involuntary attachment behavior** — the laundry panic (Ep3) and the failed attempt to untie one small knot, erasing the evidence of cost (Ep4). His refusal in Ep6 remains the series' true dark beat. Arc proof: the handover — and succeeding, for the first time, with nothing in his hands.
- **KARADI** — Large, funny, the heart. **Papa's grandson.** Keeps the nightly window lamp from Ep2 — *because Papa did*: his first lamp comes with the twenty-second source-memory. He sees Papa in the corridor (Ep5) — the boy who grieved him meets the man still protecting them. **New in v5:** he learns of Hari's lie in Ep5 (the plathi's one quiet sentence) and says nothing; he is wounded in Ep6 when the strained protection half-fails. His lamp speech is therefore **forgiveness** — the strongest register. Lamp lit Ep2 → strained Ep6 → extinguished in daylight Ep7, for the right reason.
- **RAKESH** — Believes every problem has a scientific explanation. **New in v5:** owns a running refrain — *"There's an explanation. There's always an explanation."* — repeated every episode, inverted at the rite (Ep7, tying a thorthu: "There's an explanation." *beat* "This is it."). Absorbs the valve roles: he is the **negotiator** (author of the "terms of surrender" letter, Ep5) and the **fixer** (barricade logic, Ep6). Voices the wrong hypothesis at Ep2's out ("cotton oxidizes"). His instruments log what his worldview can't hold (the well recording, Ep4; the temperature plunge, Ep5). Ends the series exactly as promised — thermometer in one hand, thorthu in the other — **now scheduled in the Ep7 grid.**
- **MEENU** — Botany student, the gang's brilliant observer — **and now a person with a stake: two years of her scholarship fieldwork is planted over the dead.** Her survey map *is* the buried cemetery map — and in v5 the discovery horrifies *her first* (Ep4): her thesis was a gravestone rubbing all along. She carries the hostel side of the Ep5 crosscut (her instruments, the aftermath of the separation attack), lays her overlay on the floor of the grandmother's house beside Kannan's history (the promised her-data-his-history collision, now staged), holds the circle in Ep7, and **retires the map** — handing it to the plathi as the record of where the dead lay.
- **KANNAN** — Scholarship student from the community; three years of keeping his head down above his own ancestors. **New in v5:** at the Ep3 fest/market he watches Hari's luck operate and *does not laugh with the others* — the suspicion thread staged, so his Ep4 stillness is confirmation, not first contact. In Ep6 the head-down years end on camera: at the boundary, he performs the small domestic gesture his grandmother taught him, publicly, for the first time. He stands with the plathi at the end. Never mystical — an agronomy student who knows what the roots are circling.
- **SIBI** *(new, minor)* — The coward-who-shows-up. Seeded by name in Ep1's water fight (first to flee, loudly). Vanishes at every scare for four episodes. Shows up in Ep6, shaking, holding a lamp. Faints in Ep7 — after it's all over (the exhale laugh).
- **THE RICH BOYS** — Not villains: privilege makes them reckless, and one terrible mistake changes everyone's lives. The reckoning stands: siege-night service, and the leader's owned admission — "We took her there."
- **THE GREEN-EYED SPIRIT** *(canonical name pending research — chosen not to fight "Pachakkanni," the handle Ep3's title hands the audience)* — Dispossessed twice: by bulldozers in Rajasthan, by the dead who use her here. She strikes only those who treated her as prey, and terrifies rather than kills. **New in v5:** her sympathy flip is *loaded*, not asserted — one near-capture inside the Ep4 parade (her POV terror as a charlatan's trap almost works: the consent/cage distinction demonstrated), and one image of homesickness misreadable as stalking (Ep4 night: outside the warden's window, watching a family at dinner). Her housing, not her defeat, is the climax.
- **THE PLATHI** — Book-ends the series. His weapon is patience: he *cannot* take the cloth (Rule 2), so he waits for the boy. He asks for it exactly once, early, quietly — and never asks again. **New in v5:** he tied Papa's first knot (Act Zero — his own handiwork; the knot that survives), and in Ep7 he receives not just the cloth but *his dead colleague*: one beat — he holds Papa's cloth a moment longer than the rite requires, touches the first knot, bows his head. The Ep5 sentence to Karadi — *"I asked your friend once. The second week."* — is a report, not a second ask; the one-question discipline holds.
- **THE CONSTABLE** — Introduced Ep3, pays off Ep6: the state's man helpless at the edge of what the state built over. His radio (Ep6) carries the second signal of scale: the same dog-madness, two districts over.
- **THE GANG'S COLLECTIVE NAME** — coined in one line of hostel slang in Ep1 (dialogue pass; Malayalam writers' room to coin). Five-friend ensembles need a name fandoms can adopt.

## 4. The Girl — Corporeality Rules (demonstrate once, never break)

Fully corporeal by daylight among the living · thins after dark and near the ground's pull · no device retains her image · cannot cross the thorthu's circle · acts only as the ground's strength allows — tilted luck early, full manifestation only from Ep6.

**Canonical demonstrations (each rule shown exactly once, then trusted):** daylight corporeality — **the daylight sighting** (Ep4: solid in a daytime crowd, seen by one boy, unremarkable to everyone else — the "ghosts come at night" assumption broken once, cheaply); image-retention — the empty frame on the TV crew's monitor (Ep4); the circle — she circles Hari's floor without entering (Ep4); **full manifestation (Ep6) is defined in the bible as intensification of stillness and wrongness — never morphology.** No creature-form exists in this show; a monster-form would kill both the gentle-horror ethic and the Ep7 housing climax.

## 6. The Season's Shape (progression check)

- **Ep 1–3:** campus comedy with a mysterious lucky object — the fun now *built* (use ladder, fest set-piece) with a countdown under it (the traveling plathi) and a price accruing (the knots).
- **Ep 3–4:** the supernatural mystery spreads through the hostel; the setting itself (plants, wells, dreams) becomes the instrument panel; the haunted discover they are also *guarded*.
- **Ep 5–6:** the hidden world opens — an erased cemetery, a country of displaced souls — the conflict moves *inside* Hari, and the cost lands on the person with the most right to the cloth.
- **Ep 7:** resolution by emotional sacrifice, not battle; then the coda promises the cycle continues.
- **Retention spine:** every episode opens on a live loop (In column) and closes on an engineered out; the two historically dangerous episodes (the lore episode, the refusal episode) now carry present-tense wounds.

## 7. Director's-hat notes

- **Act Zero:** wordless, unhurried *in register*, capped at ~2:30 *in duration* — ending on the first-knot close-up and the whisper. It buys the series its seriousness and seeds the finale in minute one. The smash cut to twenty screaming boys is the tonal contract. This scene is also the "this is not a web-skit" shelf signal — it survives every trim pass.
- **The knot bible (elevated from prop note to production law):** canonical per-episode knot targets — Ep1: 1 (the priest's, visually distinct) · Ep2 ends: 2 · Ep3: 4 · Ep4: 7 (the counting scene counts seven) · Ep5: 9 · Ep6: 16+ including one tied live on camera · Ep7: undone to 1. **One clean, well-lit audit shot of the cloth's edge per episode; continuity is absolutist** — the fan tally must be verifiable. Aging states matched to counts. Ep6 Hari unties *the first knot only*. **The Mother Courage law:** the camera counts the knots; the characters never speak of them — Hari's Ep4 counting scene is the single sanctioned break, and it plays as confession. **The hem has a capacity:** design the edge so the audience can see how much room is left; Ep6's live tie visibly crowds the cap. **Knots are signatures:** the priest's first knot is distinct because every plathi's tie is — the finale's archive read (each cloth namable) depends on this grammar being physically real in the prop.
- **Hidden Papa frames (Hill House device):** in two or three night frames across Eps 2–4, the still white figure stands unremarked, unfocused, deep in the background. Never lit, never scored, never acknowledged. The second watch becomes mandatory.
- **The parade discipline:** the parade fully precedes the third attack; each failure capped at ~90 seconds; every catcher exits through a chill. Comedy carries dread out the door — it never deflates it. The ratchet is visible (an audit shot after each catcher: darker, one knot newer), and the exorcist's on-camera challenge is the episode's hinge — staged as showbiz, answered as summons.
- **Crossroads scale:** a *queue, not an army* — three figures on three roads at dusk says more than three hundred in VFX. One far-travelled figure, exactly. *Bhoothakaalam* grammar throughout; cap designed figures.
- **Manifestation law:** the girl's Ep6 "full manifestation" is intensification of stillness and wrongness — never morphology. No creature-form exists in this show.
- **The linen turn (Ep6):** the borrowed-cloth manifestation is the one sanctioned extension of the manifestation law, and it obeys it — towels and sheets lifting and *holding*, faces implied, never formed. Stillness, not morphology, even in cloth.
- **The audible countdown (Eps 2–3):** one recurring far-off sound under every second luck win — designed, consistent, never explained. By Ep6 the audience knows what it was counting.
- **Papa is assembled, not flashbacked:** the living-Papa dose stays under two minutes (the Act Zero gesture, the lamp memory, the grandmother's sentence); everything else is posthumous portraiture — places, objects, testimony. The audience builds him; that is why they grieve him.
- **Meenu's map:** a real prop evolving across Eps 3–5; its Ep4 overlay reveal designed as a self-contained 30-second unit; retired on camera in Ep7.
- **The finale image:** hundreds of white thorthus, moving *softly* — the horror-prop flutter is banned until the coda's single cloth. Papa's branch is *the same branch* from Ep1 — schedule the shot-match.
- **The lamp motif:** Papa's lamp (Act Zero) → Karadi's lamp (lit Ep2 with source-memory, strained through the siege, extinguished in daylight Ep7). One habit, two keepers, three payoffs.
- **The corridor figure's double-read:** Ep4's glimpse is shot as horror first — white figure, classic ghost — so the head-turn recodes it. Ep7's full presence is continuous with the corridor glimpse: same stillness, no glow-up.
- **Ep7's pressing:** staged as genuine horror at arm's length (cold, breath, the crowd of the waiting) — the rite must be *survived*, not performed.
- **Three-endings guard:** kavu image → farewells (≤90 sec) → coda. The finale exhales once.
- **Title cards:** each episode title is an untranslated Malayalam noun; the card lands at the moment the word is first *earned* in-scene.
- **The sacred knot:** design the priest's first knot as a real, reproducible, teachable tie.
- **Locations:** hostel block · village + savaparambu/kavu (one schedule block: Ep1, Ep5 colony, Ep7) · market/fest day. Eps 6–7 nights boarded continuously; monsoon contingency. Period dressing: 2006 — no smartphones anywhere in frame; tape recorder, min-max thermometer, TV crew betacam, printed photographs.

## 8. Authenticity & Compliance

1. The ritual and community are fictional and declared so; the **kavu** element makes consultant review *more* necessary, not less — sacred groves are real, living institutions in Kerala. Marketing never claims "based on a real ritual." **New in v5:** Ep1 stages the *real* white-cloth funerary custom accurately and visibly apart from the invented rite — recognition anchors belief, and the boundary between real practice and fiction stays legible on screen.
2. "Gypsy" retired everywhere; the clan's identity is a research task, not an invention — **closed before any marketing asset ships.** The Ep5 flashback and the grandmother scene are the two dignity scenes — protected in every cut; the Ep5 crosscut lands its cuts *around* the grandmother's scene, never inside it.
3. Certification: target **13+**, with one red line — Ep3's luring must remain *legible* at 13+ (intent readable, nothing explicit, her control visible on rewatch), because the Ep6 reckoning is priced off what the audience understood in Ep3. **Test that scene in the certification pass; if legibility fails at 13+, take 16+ rather than gut the beat.** Injuries off-screen where possible; Karadi's Ep6 wound is aftermath, not impact. The Ep7 wavering is fear, not violation.
4. **Imitation forecast:** viewers *will* tie knots for their own dead, and some will visit real sacred groves. The on-screen rite therefore models the respectful version — no marketing invitation to hang cloth at real kavus (see §10: the campaign verb is the knot, not the hanging).

## 10. Positioning, Release & Spoiler Economy (new)

- **Sell the inversion, never terror:** "the ghost is the one protecting them" is the trailer-speakable pitch in any language. A terror-sell makes Eps 5–7 a bait-and-switch and poisons completion.

- **Protected images (never in any marketing or pre-release clip):** the corridor head-turn; any Papa manifestation; the shelter reveal; the handover; Papa crossing to the girl; the queue at scale; the daylight-hundreds composition; the coda.
