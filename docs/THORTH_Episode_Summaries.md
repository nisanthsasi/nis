## EPISODE 1 — "PAPA" (24–25 min)

**ACT ZERO (0:00–2:30, wordless).** Dusk, rain coming. A small house at the forest's edge. An old man — PAPA — lifts his window lamp and sets it closer to the glass; down on the road, a drenched stranger walks by that light, and Papa nods to him through the window, an old habit, an open door made of flame. Morning. The lamp has burned out in the window; inside, so has he — sitting where he kept the watch. The PLATHI arrives, alone; no wailing, no crowd — this is craft. He unfolds a white thorthu over Papa's chest and we watch the impossible happen matter-of-factly: the cloth settles like something exhaling into it. The plathi's hands — CLOSE-UP — tie one deliberate, distinctive knot at the corner. His lips, barely: *"until the land accepts you again."* He carries the cloth into the **kavu** — a grove of ancient trees hung with dozens of white cloths, all still — and ties it to one low branch. Hold on the branch. **[Audit: 1 knot — the priest's.]**

**SMASH CUT (2:30).** Twenty boys screaming — a hostel water fight, buckets and hoses, 2006 haircuts, a transistor blaring. Total joy. And in one wide shot nobody comments on: **every dog on campus sits motionless, silent, facing B-block.** The gang, in motion: HARI dodges the water like he dodges everything — beautifully, effortlessly; KARADI is the loudest casualty; RAKESH is trying to measure the water pressure ("There's an explanation. There's always an explanation."); SIBI flees, loudly, first. One line of hostel slang names the gang. In a corridor beat, Hari walks past the arts-fest signup sheet, pen offered, everyone certain he'd win — and doesn't stop walking.

**MINUTE 8. The telegram.** A peon crosses the wet courtyard with it: **"PAPA DIED."** Karadi reads it twice and his knees go. Hostel machinery: the bus, a wreath on Karadi's lap, the gang around him because that's what a gang is.

**The wrong-papa movement (9:00–13:00).** The village. They march the wreath to a house in mourning — and grieve, theatrically, at the wrong veranda: another Papa, three lanes over, died the same week. A child corrects them. The comedy is total and then — turning into the right lane, seeing Karadi's mother at the gate — it drains out of the scene like water, and the film's real funeral begins. **The real custom, shown accurately and apart:** relatives lay new white cloths over the body, one by one. Karadi's specific grief image: in Papa's room he finds the window lamp, wick burned to nothing, and holds it with both hands like a phone that won't ring. Somewhere at the compound's edge, glimpsed and not underlined: the plathi, watching the boys. Saying nothing.

**Cricket beside the kavu (17:00–21:00).** Restless boys, a tennis ball, stumps of firewood. Hari bats like a god who never enrolled. Then — the bite: Hari down in the grass, two red points on his ankle, and the afternoon detonates. Rakesh's mouth on the wound, spitting, shouting science; boys running in circles — and KARADI, in blind panic, sprints into the grove and grabs *the nearest hanging cloth* to bind his friend's leg. The nearest cloth. The newest one. Off the low branch.

**Hospital, one scene, 40 seconds (21:30).** The doctor, unimpressed: no venom, no snake — a thorn, probably a centipede. Go home. But Hari lies on the bench having *believed*, for twenty minutes, that he was dying — and the cloth was on the wound when the dying stopped. In the bus window's reflection he looks at it, folds it small, and puts it in his bag. Says nothing to anyone.

**DOUBLE OUT (23:00, intercut).** Night. The bus rolls through the hostel gate — **and every dog on campus erupts at once**, hurling themselves at fences, at air / the kavu, dark: the plathi stands before **one empty branch.** He does not shout. He picks up his stick and looks down the road. CUT TO BLACK.

## EPISODE 2 — "LUCKY HARI" (20 min)

**IN (0:00–0:40, wordless).** A country bus, dawn light through dust. The plathi, stick upright between his knees, riding. That's all. Every win in this episode now has a countdown under it.

**The luck ladder (0:40–9:00).** *Private and petty:* Hari wins an argument with the warden he had no business winning; the botany practical he never studied for turns out to be the one page he'd read, the *only* page. *Public:* the inter-department match — sixes, absurd sixes; then the scaffold above the mess walkway lets go and misses the entire gang by a metre — the debris settling around them in **a perfect circle**, boys untouched inside it, too shaken to notice the geometry. *"Generous":* Hari starts spending it on others — gets Sibi's fine waived, wins Karadi the mess-token bet — the theft moralizing itself as gift. **Under the montage, the design ticks:** every second or third save, a micro-wrongness — a dog going silent mid-bark; a room's sudden cold; the cloth heavier in the hand than cotton has any right to be — and each such night, in a shot nobody comments on, **the dogs face B-block a little longer.** Beneath every second win, one recurring far-off sound — a single temple bell — lands and is never explained. Deep in the background of one night frame, unfocused and unremarked: a still white figure. *(Hidden Papa frame #1.)*

**The christening (9:00).** After the sixes: "LUCKY HARI" — chalked on his door, chanted at the nets — and the **towel-flick salute** is born, a snap of cloth off the shoulder. The campus does it at him; he starts doing it back. He walks to the arts-fest sheet and writes his name. He is starting to love the man the cloth makes him.

**The kindness beat (11:00).** Cloth left folded in his room — this matters — Hari spends an evening coaching the hostel's worst singer for the fest heats, patiently, expecting nothing, enjoying it. One scene. Unguarded. (The fall, later, must fall from here.)

**The plathi's visit (13:00–15:30).** No delegation, no scene. The old man simply *is* on campus, and finds Hari alone by the cycle stand. One question, quiet as weather: **"Where is the thorth?"** Hari — a heartbeat — lies: "Lost. At the hospital." The plathi studies him for a long, level moment. And leaves. **As he passes the gate, the dogs go quiet.** From a first-floor window, KANNAN clocks the visitor — recognizes exactly what that old man is — and says nothing to anyone. Three years of keeping his head down holds.

**Karadi's lamp (16:30).** On his sill, Karadi sets a small oil lamp and lights it. Twenty seconds, to Hari, not looking at him: *"Papa kept one burning every night. His whole life. For anyone still on the road."* He leaves it lit. Rakesh, shown the cloth's odd grey cast at study hour, doesn't look up: **"Cotton oxidizes."**

**OUT (18:30).** After a save, alone, Hari spreads the cloth to fold it. It is a shade darker than yesterday. And along its edge — **a small new knot he never tied.** He looks at it a long time. HOLD. Then, through his window, in full dark: **a night-bloomer stands open where nothing should flower.** CUT. **[Audit: 2 knots.]**

## EPISODE 3 — "PACHAKKANNI" (20 min)

**IN (0:00–1:00 → 4:00): the arts-fest set-piece.** Fest day — the season's signature luck sequence. Hari performs a mono-act he barely rehearsed, and the world conspires: his rival's harmonium wheezes dead; the mic that crackled all morning goes silk for him; the judge's dropped pen rolls his way like a compliment. Every beat a whimsical contraption of tilted chance. The hall roars; the salute snaps across a hundred shoulders. On the trophy — his name, first of the list. **In the crowd, one face is not laughing: Kannan**, watching the luck operate like a man reading a familiar handwriting. *(Knot, quietly, after: 3.)*

**Pooram market (4:00–8:00).** Festival streets, loudspeakers, bangles, smoke. The nomad caravan arrives at the town's edge — and at the hostel road, **the bullocks refuse the gates**: planted feet, rolling eyes, drivers baffled. Among the caravan: THE GIRL — green-eyed, self-contained, *strangely familiar.* Kannan, buying jaggery, half-raises a hand to greet her — and stops, with no idea why. (On rewatch: she has already seen him, already moved on; every step she takes through this episode is a choice.)

**The luring (8:00–12:00).** The rich boys notice her the way they notice everything — as available. The approach is charm with money behind it; her responses read, on first watch, as naïve. (On rewatch she is steering: closer to the hostel, closer to the gate, closer to the ground's edge.) A whistle from a market lane — the caravan has missed her; a police jeep noses through the crowd (THE CONSTABLE, first proper scene, unhurried and thorough). Panicking, the boys shunt her through the back stairs **into Hari's room** — third floor, one exit, the room that sits over the old ground's rim — and lock it from outside.

**The laundry panic (12:00–14:00).** Same hour, elsewhere: the dhobi's collection has swept Hari's room. Hari discovers the thorthu *gone* — and tears through the washing compound like a man whose child is missing, down lines and lines of hanging white cloths, dozens of identical rectangles turning in the wind *(the finale's image, inverted, hidden inside a comedy beat)* — until he finds it, un-dunked, and holds it to his chest with both arms. The dhobi wants an explanation. There isn't one. **(Save on the market road — a bolting bullock parts around the gang like water: knot 4.)**

**B-story (14:30).** Meenu, in the botany plots, logs the anomaly she now has a mate for: the night-bloomer that opened at noon. Two entries, one folder: "weird." At the market's far edge, unremarked: the plathi, standing in the crowd's flow, watching the caravan pass. Wordless.

**OUT (17:30–19:30).** Evening. The constable makes the rich boys open the room. Third floor. One exit. One window, sixty feet over stone. **Empty.** Not "escaped" — *empty*, the way a shell is. That night, in his own bathroom, the first rich boy is attacked — we stay outside the door with his friends; what comes through it is not screaming but a kind of begging — and when they break it open he is unmarked, folded into the corner, **terrified.** Not killed. CUT.

## EPISODE 4 — "KAVAL" (20 min)

**IN (0:00–0:50).** The infirmary, dawn. The attacked boy, awake all night, will not say what he saw. His right fist has been clenched since they found him. When the nurse finally works it open — a crushed marigold from the market. He starts to cry.

**The hostel changes (0:50–4:00).** Symptom montage, matter-of-fact: creepers a hand-span longer overnight; dogs that will not enter the compound sitting in a rank at the gate; two rooms cold as riverbeds; boys comparing dreams over breakfast and going quiet — they have been dreaming the same old woman, and none of them has ever met her. **The well set-piece (90 sec, audio-led):** Rakesh lowers his tape recorder on a rope. Plays it back. The well answers his test-word a beat too late — and then, in a voice made of echo, says a name. The name belongs to the dreamed old woman. ("There's an explanation," Rakesh says, and for the first time it sounds like a question.)

**The ghost-catcher parade (4:00–11:00).** Hari's wallet opens — he bankrolls everything except the one real payment. **One:** Rakesh's science sting — thermometers, flour on thresholds, fishing-line trip bells — capped at ninety seconds, and it fails *through* a chill: the max-min thermometer records a 3 a.m. plunge no weather explains. **Two:** a lottery-swami with a brass plate — his camphor flame gutters sideways in a windless room; he refunds half and leaves before his tea. **Three:** the local-TV exorcist celebrity, betacam crew in tow, incense like a smoke machine. On their monitor, the corridor frame holds **an empty chair where the girl is standing.** *(Image-retention rule, demonstrated once.)* His brass-and-ash "binding trap" — theatrical junk — **almost works:** one shot from HER point of view, the circle's edge crawling closed, a beat of pure animal terror *(the consent/cage distinction, planted for Ep7)* — before it collapses into farce. **After each catcher, the audit shot: the cloth a shade darker, a knot newer — the parade measurably escalating the haunting it mocks. [5 → 6 → 7.]** The exorcist closes his segment to camera, gold-ringed hand raised: **"Come then, spirit."** A showman's mockery. An invitation.

**Daylight (11:30).** One boy from the gang, buying tea in the market crowd, sees her — solid, ordinary, sun on her shoulders, meeting his eyes without hurry — and no one else looks twice. He drops his glass. *(The "ghosts come at night" assumption, broken once, cheaply.)* That night: she stands outside the warden's quarters, watching a family at dinner through the window — a boy misreads it as a predator casing a house. On rewatch it is homesickness, plain as hunger.

**B-story (13:00–15:30).** Meenu unrolls her survey overlay across the rec-room floor and goes silent mid-sentence: **the roots under the hostel grow in perfect circles.** *As if around graves.* And it horrifies *her* first — her plots, her two years of fieldwork, planted over the dead. Kannan looks at the map and goes very still. Then, the name-layer: in her survey legend, the plot's registry name — a clerk's meaningless corruption — and Kannan, reading it aloud slowly, **restores the word underneath: the plot number *is* the savaparambu's name, misspelt into the record by the man who acquired it.** The erased ground survives inside the paperwork that erased it. Kannan, to Karadi and Hari: **"Come with me on Sunday."** — The attacks, tracked on Rakesh's wall-chart, obey rules: only the market group; and she circles Hari's floor, again and again, without entering. — Night: Hari counts the knots by torchlight, lips moving, like a man checking an account **(seven — the single sanctioned counting scene; it plays as confession)**. Then, alone, he tries to untie one small knot — to erase one entry of the evidence — and his hands will not do it. — The constable, off the record, over tea: "That clan passed here once before. My grandfather's time. Nothing happened then."

**OUT (18:00–19:30).** The night of the broadcast challenge, the third attack — **inside a locked room** (still one of the market group; she keeps her law; the exorcist's crew is gone before dawn). Shouts, running, a door giving way — and down the long corridor, KARADI, sleepless by his lamp, sees it: **a still white figure standing between the attack and the sleeping gang.** It does not move like a threat. It does not move at all. Then **it turns its head** — and it is watching *over* them. CUT.

## EPISODE 5 — "SAVAPARAMBU" (20 min)

**IN (0:00–0:45).** 4 a.m. Karadi upright by the window lamp, blanket around his shoulders, staring down the empty corridor where the figure stood. He crosses to Hari's bed. Opens his mouth. Cannot. Goes back to the lamp.

**Sunday: the colony (0:45–4:00).** A country bus (the same route the plathi rode in Ep2, reversed). Kannan brings Karadi and Hari — and a cardboard tube: Meenu's overlay, copied. Meenu and Rakesh stay behind with the instruments. The colony: small, swept, older than everything around it. The grandmother receives them on her veranda.

**The grandmother (4:00–8:00 — unbroken; the show's stillest scene, subtitled).** Unhurried, in her own dialect, she tells it: the hostel stands on their **savaparambu**; the kavu that guarded it was cut with the foundations — she was a girl; she remembers the saws. And one sentence for Papa, when Kannan names him: **"His hands tied half the cloths in that grove."** The craft is a small world; the plathis knew each other the way rivers know each other. — On her floor, Kannan unrolls Meenu's overlay: root circles over remembered graves — she touches the paper crossings one by one, naming who lies at each. Beside it, the acquisition file Kannan pulled from the village office: the ground sold "free of encumbrances," and the grandmother reads the signatory names aloud, one by one — **men who were already buried in it.** The dead were made to sign their own eviction.

**Crosscut — the hostel demonstrates (interleaved 8:00–13:00, never inside her scene).** While the colony explains the beacon, the hostel shows it working: Meenu's min-max thermometers log the cold walking up the building floor by floor; her printed photograph of the corridor develops with a gap where a girl should be. And — while the cloth is thirty kilometres away — **the fourth attack.** Discovered in aftermath on the gang's return: a market-group boy, terrified, in a room gone winter-cold. *The protection has a radius, and everyone outside it just learned the price.* **(Two strains on the journey itself — a bus that brakes a half-second early, a colony path stone that turns nobody's ankle: audit 9.)**

**The plathi completes it (13:00–15:00).** He is there — it is his community — and he finishes the story the grandmother began: a housed soul came among the homeless. **A lamp in a dark room.** The ground woke the night you carried it through the gate. The soul consents, not the cloth. — And then, quietly, to Karadi alone, as the others roll the map: **"I asked your friend once. The second week."** Karadi turns and looks at Hari across the veranda. Hari is laughing at something Kannan said. CUT AWAY. *(No scene. The lie is now living in Karadi's chest, and we carry it with him.)*

**The flashback (15:00–16:30, ≤90 sec, near-silent — dignity scene, protected).** Rajasthan. A burial ground; a bulldozer blade; a grave opened to the sun. A caravan's routes, over a century, bending — unknowingly — around grave-grounds, one girl's search steering their feet. *Homeless spirits recognize each other.*

**The reframe (16:30–18:00).** Back, at night, the gang around Hari's cot, the cloth spread between them: not a talisman. Not a prison. **Shelter.** Papa leaves it at night, at terrible cost, to protect them — Karadi's corridor figure, confirmed aloud. Every knot a night of a dead man's overtime. Hari, greyer than the cloth: he understands, completely, that **he is living in someone's afterlife.** And he still cannot let go. *(Valve, one 45-second single take: Rakesh drafts terms of surrender addressed "To Whom It May Concern (Deceased)" while the gang war over honorifics — "Respected Late Sri—" "You can't say *late*, he's *here*—")*

**OUT (19:00).** Night. Every dog on campus turns toward the foundations — and howls, together, one long note. Far off, on three roads at once, in the last light: **figures. Walking. Toward the beacon.** CUT.

## EPISODE 6 — "URUKKU" (20 min)

**IN (0:00–0:40).** The same three roads. The same framing. First light. **The figures are nearer.**

**The refusal (0:40–4:00).** The gang, the map, the file, the plathi's word — everyone knows what has to happen. And Hari says no. The show lets him say the true reason, plainly, without music: **without it, he is ordinary again.** He was nothing before this — he *chose* to be nothing, walked out of every room he could have won — and the cloth ended that. His fear is already back; his hands already shake **while the cloth is still in his hands.** *(Tolerance before surrender: the drug has stopped working, and he still won't put it down.)*

**Active inside the refusal (4:00–8:00).** He does not sulk; he *builds* — every wrong answer money and cleverness can raise. Rakesh's fixer logic: barricades, rotas, salt lines, lime, iron nails, charms bought wholesale in the market *(sincere, amateur, human)* — and at dusk the first far-travelled dead **drift through all of it like fog through a fence.** Only the cloth's own circle holds. The queue assembles at the boundary: not an army — **a queue**, which is worse. Patient. Ordered. Each soul before its own gate. In the line, one figure wears the desert iconography the flashback taught us: **verifiably not from here.** The constable's radio, static-wrapped: the same dog-madness, two districts over, and counting. The constable at the boundary with his service revolver drawn — pointed, lowered, pointed — at nothing a weapon answers.

**The pressing (8:00–13:00).** The ground fully awake. Rooms empty floor by floor; mattresses migrate to the courtyard; the hostel contracts around Hari's room. **The linen turn:** on the drying lines, towels and bedsheets lift — one by one — toward the foundations, and *hold*, bellied taut in windless air, faces implied and never formed. In a story about a towel, every towel in the building is suspect now. — **Mid-siege, in full view of the gang, a knot ties itself** — the cost, always discovered after the fact, now happening live and public — and the audit shot finds **the hem nearly full.** *(16+; the ledger's capacity, visibly running out.)* — The rich boys serve: water, watch shifts, carrying the frightened juniors' bedding, no speeches. At the boundary, the leader stands next to Kannan and gets it out, owned, unprompted: **"We took her there. Say it."** — KANNAN's beat: at the boundary stone, in front of everyone, he performs the small domestic gesture his grandmother taught him — a cupped flame set down, a murmured line in his own dialect — **the head-down years ending on camera.** Sibi shows up. Shaking. Holding a lamp.

**The half-failed save (13:00–15:00).** The protection strains past its limit: a stair collapse the cloth only half-catches — and it is **KARADI** who falls. Bandaged arm, cut brow; nothing broken; everything broken. Papa was too spent — because Hari has spent him. The refusal now has a face, and it is the face of the boy with the most right to the cloth. The gang stops asking Hari. They just look at him.

**The plathi states the stakes (15:00–16:00).** Arrived at the boundary, stick planted: if the lamp keeps burning over open ground, **every erased graveyard in the country will send its dead here.** Not from hate. From homelessness.

**Karadi at the lamp (16:30–18:00).** Bandaged, sitting by his window lamp, not looking at Hari — Karadi, who has known about the lie since the colony veranda and has said nothing, whose blood is on the stairs — says it gently, which is the worst way: *"You've seen him. He's been standing guard every night while we called him a lucky charm. Letting go isn't losing him — it's letting him finish his work."* **Forgiveness, not comfort.** Hari cannot look back at him.

**OUT (19:00).** Hari stands. Walks out of the room, down through the courtyard, past the gang, past the plathi, **into the circle** — and, kneeling, begins to untie the knot at the cloth's corner. The first knot. The priest's knot. His fingers find it — and **everything goes dark.** CUT. *(Nothing resolves before the cut.)*

## EPISODE 7 — "KETTU" (20 min)

**IN (0:00–1:00, continuous from the blackout).** Torchlight swims up. **The plathi's hands close over Hari's on the half-untied first knot.** Not pulling. Receiving. *(The door opens from the living side: the untying was surrender, and it is accepted — and Papa, free to go from this moment, has stayed.)* Around them, in the dark of the ruined ground behind the hostel, the rite is already assembling: lamps, cloth, the gang.

**The rite (1:00–8:00).** The plathi at the centre, KANNAN beside him — handing, folding, learning; the gang holding the circle, hand to hand: Meenu, Rakesh, Sibi, the rich boys, the constable at the ring's far edge with his weapon finally holstered. **No fight. No shouting. No exorcism. One ritual.** — **The circle holds by form:** complete coverage, hand to hand — and it has one gap: Karadi's bandaged arm, which cannot grip. The dead press *there*, cold flowing at the weak point like water finding a crack — the breach paid in knots on the cloth in the plathi's hands — until **HARI steps into the gap and closes it with his own body.** Protected by nothing. Holding his ground while the dead press close — staged as true horror at arm's length: cold, breath, the crowd of the waiting, faces at the edge of the lamplight that are only almost faces. *He does not run. For the first time in his life, he succeeds without help.* The handover completes in silence — the thorthu passing from his hands into the plathi's, given, not taken.

**The gathering (8:00–13:00).** Papa gathers. It looks like nothing supernatural at all, which is why it works: **the lost come to him as the living once did** — one at a time, drawn to the lamp he always kept. A roll-call, not a mass: each soul enters **its own new thorthu**, and over each tying the whisper returns — rite-language, never speech — *"until the land accepts you again."* Rakesh ties one of them, thermometer still in his other hand. **"There's an explanation."** Beat. **"This is it."**

**The wavering (13:00–15:00).** It wavers once. THE GIRL — at the circle's edge, last in her own queue after a century of walking — resists. Terrified, not defiant: terror of one more displacement, the same terror we saw in her eyes inside the charlatan's trap. And the plathi's hands **pause** on her waiting cloth: *this was never her land.* The jurisdiction question, asked in stillness at the worst possible moment. — Then **Papa's soul leaves the cloth and goes to her.** The gatherer crossing to the frightened; an old man's hand extended to a girl a century homeless. **The offer is made legible:** a cloth of her own; a branch beside his; place, and honor. Her consent, given, converts fear to blessing — *any tended ground is the land.* **She takes his hand.**

**The kavu (15:00–17:30).** The living kavu at the village savaparambu, daylight. **The image the series exists for: hundreds of white thorthus hanging from the ancient trees** — hundreds — moving *softly*, at rest. And readable: each cloth signed by its knot, each knot a plathi's hand, each hanging a name. Not a curtain of the dead — **an archive at rest.** Papa's cloth is hung last, **on the same branch that stood empty in Episode 1** — clean and white again, **every knot undone but the first.** The plathi holds it one moment longer than the rite requires; touches the first knot — his own handiwork, tied over his old colleague a season ago; bows his head. The girl's cloth hangs beside Papa's. — The exhale: it is over, the morning is ordinary, birdsong — and Sibi, who showed up, quietly faints into the leaf litter. The laugh that proves survival.

**Farewells (17:30–19:00, ≤90 seconds, no speeches).** Kannan and Hari: a handshake, wordless, longer than a handshake needs to be. The dogs: asleep in the hostel compound, flat on their sides in the sun — the season's needle reading zero. Karadi at his window, daylight: he puts the lamp out — **for the right reason** — and the smoke curls up into nothing. The arts-fest results sheet on the noticeboard: HARI — first name on the list. He reads it with nothing in his hands. He smiles like a boy.

**CODA (19:00–20:00).** The camera stays after the priest leaves. Quiet. Then: engine noise. **Bulldozers**, at the grove's far edge, workers with ropes and saws; a board going up on fresh posts: **COMING SOON — HILL VIEW PREMIUM VILLAS.** The camera pushes slowly, slowly, toward the cut zone — where one forgotten thorthu still hangs on a marked tree, in the machine path, white against the exhaust haze. Hold. Hold. **It moves.** CUT TO BLACK. *(No text card. No sting. Nothing for sale.)*
