## SLIDE 2 — THE PITCH (the hook, in one breath)

On a remote southern-Kerala shore, a kept woman serves two men — the drunk husband who owns her, and the sea-guard who loves her and pays her every week. She has been quietly saving every rupee for one thing: the morning she disappears.

Tonight, the plan goes off early.

**Kaitha** tells the story of that single night — a love scene, a murder, a body, and a fire — in **one continuous 90-minute take that never blinks.** The camera owns her the way the men do… until the last shot, when she walks out of the frame and leaves us behind with the man who thought he was building a future.

`[KEY VISUAL: full-bleed extreme close-up of her face, half in warm lamplight, half in cold moonlight — the two worlds splitting her down the middle.]`

## SLIDE 3 — LOGLINE & TAGLINE

**Logline:** Over one unbroken night on a Kerala shore, a woman who has secretly saved for years to escape her marriage kills her abusive husband, uses the lover who adores her to sink the body in the sea — then burns it all down and vanishes with the money, leaving him only a voice note.

**Tagline:** *Not all of it was a lie.*

`[KEY VISUAL: a clay rice pot, lid off, raw rice — and rolls of cash hidden in the grain. Shot from above, lit by a single lamp.]`

## SLIDE 4 — SYNOPSIS

A hot, windless night. KRISH, a sea-guard, comes up out of the surf to a hut at the end of the shore, where THANKAM waits — bath, lamp, curry, bed. He leaves her the week's roll of cash (quietly, his bribe money) for the savings pot they call "our field," and a charged phone he keeps alive for her in secret. *Soon,* he promises. *We'll go south. You and me.* She means it when she says it back. That is the tragedy.

He leaves. Her husband, RAMANAN, comes home drunk — ruined, tender for one breath about a blue boat they once owned, then violent. She endures the first blow without a sound. But when the fight reignites and the savings pot shatters across the floor — when he drops to his knees and starts stuffing *her years, her exit* into his waistband — something cold and long-prepared opens in her. She kills him.

She calls Krish. He comes. He sees his own money in the blood, understands everything in half a second — and chooses not to. He sinks the body past the third bar, in uniform, on his own boat. And while he is out on the black water, she packs, takes the money, douses the hut, climbs to the cliff, and sends him three words before she throws the lamp and walks out of the world he was certain they'd share.

`[KEY VISUAL: triptych — the lamp; the spilled money in rice; the burning hut seen small from the cliff.]`

## SLIDE 5 — THE FORM IS THE STORY

# 90 minutes. One shot. No cuts.

The single take is not a stunt. It is the meaning.

An unbroken shot is **surveillance** — the camera owns Thankam the way the men own her: the lover's eye, the husband's eye, ours. For ninety minutes she cannot escape the frame. Then, in the final moments, **she walks out of it** — and the camera, like the man on the beach, is left behind.

The audience doesn't just watch her escape. **We are the thing she escapes.**

`[KEY VISUAL: a long lateral push — motion-blurred — of the camera trailing her through the hut; the shot literally chasing her.]`

## SLIDE 6 — TONE & LOOK

- **Full night.** Two palettes at war: **cold** (moon, distant sodium street-lights, the cruel sea) vs. **warm** (the kerosene lamp, the hearth — the comfortable lie of "home"). The only truly bright frame in the film is the hut **burning.**
- **The camera is a consciousness,** not a coverage machine — it hands off from man to woman in the first minute and never leaves her again.
- **Violence in shadow.** The killing plays as shadow-puppetry on the lamplit wall — restrained, unforgettable, and censor-safe.
- **Sound as scale:** the sea masks the world; one boat engine; one fire; near-silence.

`[KEY VISUAL: a 4-frame mood strip — moonlit bath silhouette / two shadows folded on a wall / a torch beam across screwpine / the gold bloom of the burning hut.]`

## SLIDE 7 — CHARACTERS

**THANKAM** — the architect. Warm on the surface, doing arithmetic underneath. She loves Krish *and* she is going to leave him; both are true, fused forever. Not a victim, not a femme fatale — a woman buying her freedom with the only currencies she's been allowed: her body, her savings, and finally, betrayal.

**KRISH** — the sea-guard who adores her. A corrupt officer with a good heart, which is the most dangerous kind. He damns himself for her gladly, recognizes his own bribe money in the blood, and looks away on purpose. *Soon.*

**RAMANAN** — the husband. A strong man drink rubbed soft and mean. One breath of ruined tenderness (a blue boat, a better life) makes his violence human, and his death cost something.

`[KEY VISUAL: three night-lit portraits, each lit by a different source — Thankam by lamp, Krish by torch/boat-lamp, Ramanan by the dying hearth.]`

## SLIDE 8 — DIRECTOR'S STATEMENT

*"I wanted to make a film you cannot look away from — literally, because there is nowhere to cut to. For ninety minutes the audience is trapped in the frame with Thankam, complicit in watching her, the way everyone in her life has watched and weighed and wanted her. The unbroken take is the cage. The last shot is the only door.*

*This is a thriller, but it is really about ownership — of a woman's body, her labour, her money, her silence — and about the terrible arithmetic a person does when freedom has a price and the only way to pay it is in love. I want the audience to leave arguing about her. To not be sure what she meant. To rewatch it and find the whole plan was visible the entire time, hidden in the rice."*

`[KEY VISUAL: a single production still — the director and DP rehearsing the camera move with the lead actress, handheld rig, dark shore.]`

## SLIDE 9 — THE METHOD (and why it's affordable)

A true single take is **cheap in equipment and expensive in rehearsal** — and rehearsal is the one resource an independent film can actually afford.

- **One location** (a buildable hut + shore + cliff as a continuously traversable set), **three actors,** **one camera.**
- No coverage, no big lighting packages — practical kerosene + moon + hidden LED augmentation.
- **6–8 weeks of rehearsal → a handful of full-night attempts.** We shoot the whole film, all 90 minutes, in a night; we do it across consecutive nights; we choose the best complete performance.
- Modern low-light cinema cameras (Sony FX3 / A7S III class) make available-light night capture real.

**Precedent:** *Victoria* (2015) — one genuine 138-minute take, made for roughly €150k, won the Berlinale Silver Bear and launched its makers. The model exists. We are building on it.

`[KEY VISUAL: an overhead diagram of the continuous camera path — a figure-eight through hut/kitchen/yard, then the line up the rocks to the cliff.]`

## SLIDE 10 — THEMES / WHY IT MATTERS

- **The economy of a woman's life** — body, labour, savings, silence, all priced and owned.
- **Love as both the cage and the key** — the one true thing she has is the thing she must betray to be free.
- **Complicity** — the camera makes the audience the watcher; the ending indicts the look.

It sits inside the global Malayalam new wave of unflinching domestic-patriarchy cinema (*The Great Indian Kitchen*) — but weaponised as a real-time thriller.

## SLIDE 16 — THE CULT / COMMUNITY ENGINE

The film is **built to be rewatched and argued about.**

- **Theory-fuel:** *Not all of it was a lie* — which part? Did she ever love either man? Was the murder really unplanned? The ending refuses to resolve.
- **Hidden-in-plain-sight rewatch:** the whole plan is visible from the first scene (the arithmetic in her face, the pot, the secret phone, "soon").
- **The making-of as a franchise:** the single-take rehearsal-and-shoot process is its own documentary, its own press story, its own behind-the-scenes content stream.
