# KAITHA — Key Art / Poster Directions
*Four distinct concepts. Generate the image clean (no text), then set the title + tagline in design. Locked palette: cold (moon / sodium / sea) vs. one warm source (kerosene / fire). Title: thin restrained serif, "KAITHA · കൈത", with a single screwpine thorn-leaf. Taglines: "One woman. One night. One shot." / "Not all of it was a lie."*

> Tooling notes: prompts are tool-agnostic. For Midjourney add `--ar 2:3 --style raw`; for Flux/SDXL use the negative-prompt block at the end. Generate the hero at 2:3 (one-sheet), then re-frame for 16:9 (festival/landing) and 1:1 (social).

---

## DIRECTION 1 — "THE EXIT" (recommended primary)
**The thesis image: she walks out of the frame; the warm lie burns behind her.** This is the whole film in one poster — the gaze loses her.

**Why it wins:** instantly legible, emotional, and sells the noir + the formal idea (a lone figure leaving a single warm light in a cold world). Strong negative space top for title.

**Prompt:**
> Cinematic arthouse-noir film poster, a remote southern Kerala seashore at night, vast flat silver sea under a hard high half-moon, a single low thatched hut on black volcanic rock far below engulfed in warm golden fire, the only warm light in an entirely cold blue-silver frame, a thin distant necklace of orange sodium street lights on the far coastline, in the foreground on a dark cliff a lone woman in a simple Kerala mundu seen from behind, a cloth bundle on her shoulder, mid-stride walking away into darkness, thorny screwpine (pandanus) plants along the cliff edge, high-contrast chiaroscuro, anamorphic widescreen depth, deep crushed shadows, restrained elegant A24-style composition, photographic, large empty sky for title, 2:3 poster ratio, no text.

**Title/tagline:** title upper third in the cold sky; tagline "One woman. One night. One shot." small at base.

---

## DIRECTION 2 — "THE RICE POT" (iconographic / mystery)
**The object that holds the whole secret.** Minimal, premium, and seeds the central image without spoiling anything.

**Why it wins:** A24/Criterion-grade restraint; pure mystery-fuel (what's hidden in the grain?); ownable icon for merch/social. The least literal, most "festival" of the four.

**Prompt:**
> Minimalist arthouse film poster, overhead top-down shot of a worn earthen clay rice pot, lid removed, filled with raw white rice, and tucked into the grain like hidden eggs several tight rolls of old banknotes bound in rubber bands, lit by a single warm kerosene flame from one side casting a long soft shadow, everything else falling into deep black, intimate, tactile, grain and texture, painterly chiaroscuro still-life, warm amber light against near-total darkness, lots of negative black space around the pot for title, photographic, 2:3 poster ratio, no text.

**Title/tagline:** title in the black field above the pot; tagline "Not all of it was a lie." beneath.

---

## DIRECTION 3 — "TWO WORLDS" (character-forward / ambiguous)
**Her face, split between the warm lie and the cold truth.** Sells the protagonist and the moral ambiguity — is she victim or architect?

**Why it wins:** a face sells a character drama; the cool/warm split *is* the theme on one image; unforgettable in a thumbnail grid (OTT/streaming art).

**Prompt:**
> Cinematic film poster, tight extreme close-up of a South Indian woman in her thirties, dark skin, wet hair, a faded thin gold marriage chain at her throat, a thread of dried blood at the corner of her mouth, her expression unreadable — neither fear nor grief, something calculating and still — her face lit precisely down the centre: one half in warm amber kerosene lamplight, the other half in cold blue moonlight, total darkness behind her, fine skin texture, beads of water, high-contrast portrait, anamorphic, restrained arthouse-noir, photographic, negative space at top, 2:3 poster ratio, no text.

**Title/tagline:** title across the dark top; tagline small near her jaw on the cold side.

---

## DIRECTION 4 — "THE GIANTS" (dread / noir genre)
**Two huge shadows on a lamplit mud wall — intimacy, then the kill.** Leans hardest into thriller/genre and the film's recurring shadow-motif.

**Why it wins:** pure unease, genre-forward (best for thriller-leaning markets and the elevated-horror crossover); the cheapest-feeling concept to also shoot as a real production still.

**Prompt:**
> Cinematic noir film poster, interior of a dark mud-walled Kerala hut lit only by a single kerosene lamp on the floor, the lamp flame in sharp foreground, on the rough plaster wall behind it two enormous distorted human shadows looming and trembling — ambiguous, could be an embrace or a struggle — warm amber light fighting deep black shadow, a cracked clay pot and scattered rice on the floor catching a glint of light, oppressive intimate atmosphere, grain, chiaroscuro, anamorphic, restrained arthouse-horror tone, vertical negative space, 2:3 poster ratio, no text.

**Title/tagline:** title low and tight; tagline above the shadows.

---

## Usage & variants
- **One-sheet (print/festival catalog):** Direction 1 hero, 2:3.
- **Streaming/OTT thumbnail:** Direction 3 (a face reads at thumbnail scale); also crop to 16:9.
- **Social / teaser tile:** Direction 2 (the pot) at 1:1 — most "scroll-stopping."
- **Genre/midnight markets:** Direction 4.
- Generate 4–6 variations per prompt; pick, then up-res; composite the title treatment last so the type never fights the image.

### Negative prompt (Flux / SDXL)
> text, letters, watermark, logo, title, captions, bright daylight, sunny, cheerful, oversaturated, neon, modern city skyline, CGI plastic look, deformed hands, extra fingers, lowres, jpeg artifacts, busy cluttered composition.

### Title-treatment notes
- Typeface: a thin, high-contrast serif (e.g., a Didone) — elegance against the grime.
- Lockup: "KAITHA" with "കൈത" small beneath; a single screwpine thorn-leaf replacing or threading one stroke.
- Color: bone-white or pale amber type on the cold areas; never on the warm/fire.
