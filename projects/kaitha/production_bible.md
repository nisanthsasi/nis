# KAITHA — Production Bible
### The working document for cast & crew
*Feature · Malayalam · ONE CONTINUOUS 90-MINUTE TAKE · full night*
*Companion to: `BIBLE.md` (development), `screenplay_opening.md` (script), `pressure_test.md` (notes), `pitch_deck.md`, `key_art.md`.*

> **The single rule that governs everything in this document:** there are no cuts. The film is one unbroken 90-minute take in real time. Every department plans for *continuity within a living shot*, not for coverage. If a choice can't survive an unbroken take, it's the wrong choice.

## 2. STORY (real-time, with timecodes)
A windless, hot night. **0:00–0:05** KRISH walks up out of the surf; screwpine snags his leg; he washes at the hut's water vessels; a woman calls — *it's me*. The camera leaves him and finds **THANKAM** bathing in the moon: the hand-off. **0:05–0:18** the domestic ritual — lamp, hearth, curry, the warm "home." **0:18–0:28** they make love; he leaves the week's roll of (bribe) cash for the savings pot they call "our field," swaps her a secret charged phone (*Anyone comes — call me. Only me.*), promises *soon* — south, together. She means it back. He leaves.

**0:28–0:42** RAMANAN comes home drunk — the husband. One breath of ruined tenderness (a blue boat they once owned), then violence. The first blow: **she does not react.** He goes to wash. **0:42–0:55** he returns; she retaliates for the first time; in the struggle the savings pot shatters and the money spills; he drops to his knees and starts stuffing her years into his waistband — and she takes a billet of firewood and **kills him** (shadow-play on the wall). **MIDPOINT 0:55.**

**0:55–1:05** genuine horror, then cold clarity returns; she recovers the money, calls Krish on the secret phone. **1:05–1:15** he comes, sees his own bribe money in the blood, understands — and *chooses not to*; he wraps the body, dons his uniform, motors it out past the third bar. The camera stays on the shore with her. **1:15–1:25 THE SOLO:** alone, she packs, gathers every note off the bloodied rice, douses the hut in kerosene, climbs the rocks (the kaitha takes its tax from her too). **1:25–1:29 CLIMAX:** from the cliff she records a voice note — *"Not all of it was a lie."* — sends it, and throws the lamp; the hut blooms gold. The boat returns; his small figure by the fire lifts the phone. **1:29–1:30:** she **walks out of the frame**; we don't follow. The one cut: to black.

## 3. THEME & TONE (for everyone on set)
- **Ownership.** A woman's body, labour, money, silence — all priced and owned by the men around her. The camera is the final owner; the last shot is her escaping it.
- **Love as cage and key.** The love is real on both sides — that's why the betrayal costs. Never play her as a cold schemer or a pure victim; she is **both at once, fused.**
- **The warm light is the lie.** Comfort, home, tenderness = the trap. The truth is cold (the sea, the moon) and, finally, the fire.
- **Restraint.** Violence in shadow; emotion held, not spilled; the sea covers the silence. Nothing is underlined.

## 4. CHARACTER BIBLES & REAL-TIME ARCS  *(actors' primary text)*

> For a single take, an actor cannot "find it in the edit." Each role below gives you a **biography**, the **psychology**, and a **beat-by-beat real-time map**: your *objective* (what you're doing) and your *private truth* (what's actually going on underneath, often hidden from the other characters) at each timecode. Track the throughline unbroken. Note the **Information Map** (§4.4) — what your character knows at each moment is the engine of the whole film.

### 4.1 THANKAM — the architect *(lead; the role of a career)*
**Casting:** Malayali woman, 30s, dark-skinned (per script), capable of profound **stillness** and a vast interior life that the camera can read with no dialogue; the stamina and control to carry one unbroken 90-minute performance (theatre training a strong asset); fluent, idiomatic Malayalam.

**Biography:** Married young to Ramanan when he was a strong man with a blue boat and a future. The sea took the boat; drink took the man. Years of being beaten, used, and provided-for-by-no-one taught her one lesson: **no one will free her but herself.** She began, quietly, to build an exit — taking a sea-guard, Krish, as a lover who pays, and hoarding every rupee in a rice pot. She did not plan to fall in love with him. She did anyway. The plan didn't change.

**Psychology**
- **Want (external):** vanish, with the money, before dawn — be free.
- **Need (internal):** to stop pricing herself by what men give and take; to *own herself.*
- **Wound:** every relationship she's had has been ownership; love has always meant a cage.
- **Flaw:** she believes safety requires absolute solitude — that to be free she must betray even the love that is real.
- **Contradiction:** she genuinely loves Krish **and** always meant to leave him; she nurtures (curry, care, the kept savings) the very men she's escaping.
- **Secret she carries the whole film:** tonight is the night. Everything warm she does is true *and* is the last time.

**Real-time arc map**
| TC | Objective (doing) | Private truth (underneath) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:05–0:18 | Make a warm home; receive her lover | This is one of the last nights; the warmth is real and she is already saying goodbye to it |
| 0:18–0:28 | Love Krish; keep the ritual of the pot | The "our field" is *her* field; the flashes of "arithmetic" are her counting how close she is to free |
| 0:28–0:42 | Manage a dangerous animal; give nothing | Endure. The stone face is a discipline learned over years. Do **not** play fear — play *absence* |
| 0:42–0:55 | First time: push back. Then: protect the money | The kill is **not** rage at the beating — it's the cold door opening when he grabs her exit. She'd take a beating; she will not lose the years |
| 0:55–0:59 | Survive the horror of what she's done | Real grief — she killed the blue-boat man. The one torn sound. Then the architect *returns* (not arrives) |
| 0:59–1:05 | Solve the problem; summon Krish | Hold her voice level — the hardest act of the night. She is reaching into the one clean thing and pulling it into blood, and she knows it |
| 1:05–1:15 | Let Krish believe; get the body gone | She lets the bruise tell a story she doesn't quite tell. Every "soon" she gives him is a lie she means in the moment |
| 1:15–1:25 | Pack, take everything, prepare the fire | The choice, finally visible: she loves him, she is going anyway. A hardening, not a melodrama. One honest touch — for the money, not the man |
| 1:25–1:30 | Send the words; burn it; leave | "Not all of it was a lie" is the truest and most withholding thing she can give him. Then she walks out of the frame — out of the gaze, out of ownership, free |

**Physicality/voice:** economical, unhurried, a woman who has done every task ten thousand times; clipped, low speech; warmth she can switch on and off like the lamp. **Traps to avoid:** playing villain (she isn't), playing victim (she escapes that), or signalling the plan too early (the audience should *re-read* her, not read her).

### 4.2 KRISH — the lover / sea-guard
**Casting:** Malayali man, late 30s, sea-weathered, with a **warm, boyish openness** that hides a compromised man; physically able (boat-handling, lifting a body); fluent Malayalam; must land both devotion and the single most important beat — the half-second he *chooses not to understand.*

**Biography:** A coastal sea-guard who long ago started taking money to not see the night boats ("the Lankan boats"). Lonely, decent in his own telling, he fell for Thankam and turned the corruption into a courtship — bringing her the rolls weekly, "for our field," dreaming of land down south. He charges her contraband phone himself. He believes, completely, that the savings are *theirs* and that this is a love story with a future.

**Psychology**
- **Want:** take her away; build a life together.
- **Need:** to be *chosen,* not used.
- **Flaw:** devotion blinds him; he reads her care as love *for him.*
- **Contradiction:** a guardian of the coast who becomes a body-disposer for love; an officer who is himself a criminal.
- **The key beat (1:07):** he sees his own bribe rolls in the blood; the whole truth is right there (Ramanan died over the money, not the bruise); **he folds the thought up and puts it away** because finishing it would stop him saving her. Play the *choosing*, not ignorance.

**Real-time arc map**
| TC | Objective | Private truth |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Come home to her | The happiest man on the coast; sure of his future |
| 0:18–0:28 | Provide; plan the escape together | He thinks he's building a bridge to a shared life; "soon" is a promise he believes |
| 1:05–1:09 | Save her; make it not have happened | The law never gets a word in; he is *glad* to finally be of use to her |
| 1:07 | (see/not-see the money) | The truth offers itself; he refuses it, on purpose, out of love |
| 1:09–1:15 | Dispose of the body cleanly | He thinks tonight binds them forever. He has no idea he's the price |
| (1:28, by the fire) | Receive the voice note | The bridge collapses; every "soon" goes out with the tide |

**Physicality/voice:** easy, tactile, a toucher; warm and teasing on the phone; competent and practical with the body and boat. **Trap:** don't play him stupid — play him *in love past the truth.*

### 4.3 RAMANAN — the husband
**Casting:** Malayali man, 50s (reads older), a once-powerful body gone soft with drink; must carry **ruined tenderness** as fully as menace; fluent Malayalam.

**Biography:** Owned a blue fishing boat and a place on the shore and a wife who sat in the bow while the village watched. The sea took the boat; arrack took the rest. He has nothing now but the claim of ownership over Thankam and the hut — the last things he can call his.

**Psychology**
- **Want:** dominion — food, sex, the house, control.
- **Need (unmet):** to *matter;* to be feared because being feared is the last proof he exists.
- **Flaw:** entitlement and violence.
- **Contradiction:** provides nothing, claims everything; one true flicker of the man he was.
- **Function:** the detonator. His discovery of the money at 0:48 is the spark; his death at 0:55 is the hinge.

**Real-time arc map**
| TC | Objective | Private truth |
|---|---|---|
| 0:28–0:34 | Be fed; be home | For one breath, the blue-boat man surfaces — he half-remembers loving her |
| 0:34–0:42 | Make her *be here* / fear him | Her absence (not another man) is unbearable; he hits her to feel real. Shame, not strategy |
| 0:42–0:48 | Reassert himself | The push shocks him — a flicker of fear/respect, then rage |
| 0:48–0:55 | Take the money | A drunk's small immediate mind: *mine.* He never thinks "where from"; he never thinks of Krish |

**Physicality/voice:** the heavy, dignified-not-to-fall walk of the functional drunk; slurred, fond-then-vicious; hands that were once a fisherman's. **Trap:** never a cartoon brute — the audience must, for one second, mourn him.

## 5. THE SINGLE-TAKE PERFORMANCE METHOD *(actors + AD)*
- **The film is a play you shoot.** Expect **6–8 weeks of rehearsal**, blocking the entire 90-minute journey to muscle memory — moves, props, marks, light cues, the camera's dance.
- **Stamina & throughline.** Each take is a full 90-minute unbroken performance. Build the emotional arc like a stage actor; you cannot "drop in" to a beat — you arrive at it from everything before it.
- **Resets between takes.** Art, props, HMU (blood/wet/sweat), and wardrobe reset completely between attempts (see §6). Plan **one, maybe two full attempts per night**, across **~10–14 nights**; the best *complete* performance wins, not the best moment.
- **Recovery beats.** The script's quiet stretches (the ritual, the solo) are also the actors' breathing room — use them; don't fill them.
- **Safety choreography is rehearsed cold** (the DV, the kill, the fire, the water) long before a camera rolls (§6.12).

## 6. DEPARTMENT NOTES

### 6.1 Direction
Hold the through-line of ownership and the warm-lie/cold-truth scheme in every choice. Protect restraint: no underlining, violence in shadow, emotion held. The director's hardest job is **rhythm across 90 unbroken minutes** — the modulation from intimacy → dread → horror → elegy without a cut to lean on. Decide the camera's "character" (its curiosity, its complicity) with the DP and keep it consistent.

### 6.2 Cinematography & Camera (`DP-01`)
- **Rig:** a gimbal/stabiliser build (DJI Ronin-class) with quick low/high modes; the operator is effectively a dancer who's rehearsed the whole film. Consider a trained operator + focus puller as a two-person unit moving as one.
- **Camera body:** low-light cinema/hybrid (Sony FX3 / FX6 / A7S III class) — clean high-ISO is non-negotiable for moon + kerosene night.
- **Lenses:** one fast prime bias (a 24mm or 28mm at T1.5–2) for the intimate, traversable interior; consider a vintage/anamorphic character for flares off the lamp/fire. Lock one lens language; don't swap mid-take.
- **The path (locked):** shore → hut front (vessels) → main room → kitchen → backyard → room (a figure-eight), then later out → rocks/kaitha → cliff. The set must be **continuously traversable** (see §6.4).
- **The two hand-offs:** Krish→Thankam (0:02–0:04) and Thankam→(left behind) at the end; the camera "becomes a looker" at the bath and is "abandoned" at the finale.
- **Focus** is the hardest technical risk on a moving low-light oner — rehearse marks exhaustively; consider subtle on-set distance aids.

### 6.3 Lighting Plan
- **Sources, all motivated:** the **moon** (a large soft cool keylight/ambience), the **distant sodium street-lights** (a far warm-cool accent, never reaching the hut), the **kerosene lamp** (the warm interior key — practical, dimmable via wick, augmented with hidden tiny LED/“tweenie” bulbs matched to flame colour), the **hearth**, and finally the **fire** (the climax light cue).
- **Continuity within the take:** the lamp is *carried and placed* by Thankam — its position is a real lighting instrument the actor controls; rehearse its placements as light cues.
- **The fire** is both production-design and the biggest lighting moment — plan its build, its safety (§6.12), and how its glow reaches the cliff for the finale.
- Keep an unbroken exposure logic: the camera travels from lamp-warm interiors to moon-cold exteriors in one move; ride it or pre-set so it self-balances.

### 6.4 Production Design & Art (`PD-01`)
- **The set *is* the camera path.** Build the hut + yard + a stretch of shore + the rock/kaitha climb + the cliff as **one continuous, walkable geography** (likely a built set on a real shore, or a studio-shore hybrid for the fire/safety).
- **The hut interior** (single room doing many jobs): a neatly-made bed (also the "visitors' seat"), **shelves kept obsessively straight** (her one domain of control — a key motif; dress them with precise, humble order), a draped dead **television** (no power — a shrine, not an appliance), the **kitchen** with a clay hearth and logs.
- **Hero set-pieces:** the clay **rice pot** (must break convincingly on cue — multiples), the **kerosene lamp** (multiples; throwable stunt version for the finale), the **water vessels** out front.
- **The wreck:** rice + blood + broken pot must be reset perfectly between takes — pre-build dressing kits.
- **Texture:** mud plaster, salt, kerosene smoke, humidity. The straightness of her shelves vs. the chaos that erupts is a designed contrast.

### 6.6 Costume / Wardrobe
- **Thankam:** a worn mundu + blouse; wet from the bath (continuity), then lived-in through the night; she changes once. The **thali** at her throat (reveal prop). Final: a packed bundle with a second mundu/blouse.
- **Krish:** civilian (salt-and-diesel sea clothes) for the visit; **sea-guard uniform** for the disposal — the irony is in the change. Continuity: wet legs (opening wash), then wet/sandy from wading.
- **Ramanan:** shabby, drink-worn; one set, progressively disordered through the fight.
- **Continuity across takes:** blood, water, sweat, kerosene, sand, ash — build duplicate wardrobe sets per attempt; this is a major wardrobe-continuity job on a oner.

### 6.7 Hair & Makeup
- **Hot-night sweat** sheen throughout (consistency across a 90-min take).
- **Thankam:** wet hair from the bath drying realistically over the take; the **bruise/split lip progression** — it appears at the first blow (0:37) and must read correctly from then on; blood on hands after the kill; ash/soot by the cliff.
- **Ramanan:** drink-flush; then the kill aftermath (handled mostly in shadow — coordinate with how much blood the camera actually sees).
- **Krish:** minimal; wet/sandy.
- **Resets:** full HMU reset between attempts; pre-mixed blood and a bruise-application time built into the night schedule.

### 6.8 Sound
- **Live, continuous production sound is the spine** — and the hardest job on a moving night oner with sea wind. Plan a mix of **planted/boundary mics** through the set, a discreet boom where the path allows, and **radio mics** on all three actors. The sea usefully **masks** the absence of cuts and some noise — design with it.
- **Dialogue intelligibility is the §16 hard gate** — protect the quiet interior lines especially.
- **Design beds:** the constant sea; the distant woman's call; the night insects/heat; the boat engine (start, recede, return); the fire's build; specific silences.
- Plan for minimal ADR (a oner resists it) — get it clean live.

### 6.9 Music / Score (`COMPOSER`)
- **One ownable identity** (a single instrument/era — e.g., a lone, breathing drone or a sparse stringed motif). A **≤3-note motif** that passes the "ringtone test" and can travel as a release track (the film's portable artifact).
- **Where it enters:** sparingly — let the sea and silence carry most of it; the score arrives at the turn (the kill), the solo, and the fire. The warm domestic stretch may be near-scoreless (the lie is "comfortable" and quiet).
- Deliver a stand-alone track for the campaign (§ pitch deck / cult engine).

### 6.10 Editing / Post (`ED-01`)
- **There is one cut: to black, at the end.** "Editing" here is (a) choosing the best *complete* take, and (b) shaping sound/score/grade over it.
- Within the take, watch the §16 sustain risks (the solo, the simmer) when selecting the take — pick the performance whose real-time tension never slacks.

### 6.11 Grade & VFX
- **Grade** enforces the cool/warm signature and any locked texture (a fine film grain/halation) globally; the show-LUT is set on-set so the operator sees the look live.
- **VFX is minimal:** possible safe-fire augmentation, rig/shadow/safety-wire cleanup, sky/horizon tidying, the body. Budget it as cleanup, not spectacle (use AI-assisted cleanup tools).

### 6.12 Stunts, Safety & Intimacy *(mandatory protocols)*
- **Intimacy coordinator** for the love scene (0:18) — choreographed, consented, closed set; remember the design intent is the *indicted gaze*, not titillation.
- **Domestic-violence choreography:** a fight director stages the blows and the kill so they're safe and repeatable across many takes; the kill plays in **shadow** (less physical risk, censor-safe).
- **Fire safety:** the hut burn is the highest-risk element — licensed pyro/SFX supervisor, fire crew standing by, almost certainly the **finale fire is a controlled/▼staged element** (possibly a separate rig or a built shell for the burn) coordinated with the single-take logic; the thrown lamp uses a safe practical.
- **Night sea + boat:** marine safety, life jackets out of frame, a safety boat, water-rescue standby; the lead body-double for the wrapped bundle is a weighted dummy.
- **Welfare:** night shoots, real water, real fire, heavy emotional content — schedule recovery, a welfare lead, and an on-call medic.

## 10. KEY RISKS & MITIGATIONS
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Focus/operating error mid-take | Exhaustive rehearsal; marks; two-person camera unit; many attempts |
| Dialogue lost to wind/sea | Planted + radio mics; design *with* the sea; protect interior lines |
| Continuity across resets (blood/water/fire) | Duplicate wardrobe; dressing kits; HMU reset time built into schedule |
| Fire safety | Licensed pyro; fire crew; staged/controlled burn; safe throwable lamp |
| Night sea / boat | Marine safety, safety boat, weighted dummy, life jackets out of frame |
| Actor stamina (90-min performance) | Stage-style rehearsal; recovery beats; 1–2 takes/night max |
| Tonal modulation over one take (§16/F9) | Director + composer map the rhythm; the score arrives only at turns |
| SME plausibility (body resurfacing, Krish-as-suspect) | Resolve in final script polish; treat as deliberate dread, not convenience |
